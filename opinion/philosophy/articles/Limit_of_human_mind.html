The Limit of the Human Mind

Reflections on the limits of human thought and understanding.

CuriousPanda • 3 min read

“When Aristotle drew up his table of categories which to him represented the grammar of existence, he was really projecting the grammar of the Greek language on the cosmos.” We have been so accustomed to hearing the limitless potential of the human mind and how there’s something so fundamentally different about our species. However, I believe that it could not be further from the truth—it takes a unique form of humility to acknowledge our imperfections. After all, such lucid self-awareness is the beginning of greater learning.

Let’s consider a thought experiment, shall we? I have always been heavily invested in the debate regarding the existence of God. Specifically, the key argument is that something cannot come from nothing. By extension, there must be a creator, whom we humans cannot fathom, and we define this supernatural being as God. While different religions have different interpretations of God, its fundamental purpose remains largely similar. Does this sound like Aristotle? Based on our understanding of creation and our vocabulary, we have associated an idea to a meaning. The key question then—is it certainly accurate? Perhaps the ceiling of its accuracy is merely the sophistication of our language.

Dissecting it deeper, I argue that many truths are clouded simply by the limits of our mind. If it is indeed true that any being requires a creator, then what created the creator? Now, this sounds similar to sequences and series in Mathematics, doesn’t it? In fact, we now know that certain infinite series converge to give us a finite value—are we able to apply this idea to the creator of a creator? Maybe—it could either be possible with theoretical advancements in the future, or simply beyond the understanding of humans. Just as humans are able to understand convergence to a finite value, perhaps the truth of an infinite chain of creators is solely fathomable to a higher-order species with greater intelligence and insight.

After all of these, what exactly is the point I’m trying to make? It is not my joy to underscore the limits of humanity—in fact, I have always marvelled at the ingenuity and tenacity of our ancestors as we emerged as the top of the food chain in the entire world. As the world heralds the advent of artificial intelligence and maybe even superhuman intelligence in the future, it feels unnerving that many attribute our future prosperity to the “uniqueness” of homo sapiens. “AI will never replace us because there is something special about humanity that is unable to be described through science or language.” However, I ask you one question—if science and language, the core pillars invented by humanity, are unable to describe what’s special about humanity, are you putting all of your hope on something that doesn’t exist, or something we cannot comprehend and may never be able to utilize?

Ultimately, humans are made of elements, just like any other creature. It is the same 118 elements that also create all other organisms but we believe we innately possess superior qualities. What is stopping us from being taken over by AI, or other creatures that are also formed from the same elements? Perhaps it is time we stopped assuming that all will fall nicely in place when the time comes, else we may realise that AI is the disciple we have been nurturing to replace the master.

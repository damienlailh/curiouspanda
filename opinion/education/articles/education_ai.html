Education and AI

How AI is revolutionising our education system--understand how it works or fall behind.

CuriousPanda • 10 min read

From giving students a ‘0’ score for utilising AI to distributing premium AI tools, NTU is one of many universities grappling with the education revolution sparked by AI. However, at this point whereby AI is already inextricably integrated into the student’s toolbelt, the question is not when AI would be integrated into our education system, but how we can best utilise AI to fulfil our educational needs. Ultimately, AI is merely a tool whose impact is contingent on our ability to wield it appropriately.

One of the most pressing concerns is the over-reliance on AI by students. When students receive a question or problem, many have now developed the knee-jerk reaction to dump it into AI. We no longer think critically about the problem—and that’s precisely the issue many education professionals are worried about! It’s not the answer that matters, but the process of obtaining the outcome that is enriching. If you recall in the days before AI proliferation, when we try to search for a particular source or article, we often stumble upon another interesting article that expands our perspectives. We thought thoroughly about different approaches to the specific problem and this was what made the learning particularly impactful and mind-etching. As we have always heard, “nothing great comes easy”. Unfortunately, AI has given students an easy way out by offering satisfactory answers all at their fingertips.

Perhaps the crucial responsibility now lies with educators in shaping curriculum that students are actually passionate about learning. Indeed, it is no surprise that there are numerous “redundant” lessons that students are mandated to go through and it is likely that these are precisely the lessons that have become a formality with the use of AI. When students are unable to see the true value in exploring certain areas or listening in particular classes, AI now becomes an easy way for students to cruise through these “unimportant” lessons and direct their attention to those they are passionate about. Before we immediately start demonising students for taking this shortcut, perhaps we should first reflect on our education system as a whole—why do such lessons, despite the disinterest of students, remain so ingrained in our curriculum? Are there better ways of approaching these areas rather than simple assignments which prove to only expand the AI’s database rather than our students’ knowledge? AI can be seen as a threat to our conventional education, but it may be simultaneously an opportunity for everyone to rethink our education system as a whole.

In addition, I recall reading about an article which highlighted the future roles that AI is unlikely to replace given the rising fears of AI displacing our workforce—some examples include teachers, business executives and healthcare workers. A common trait is the empathy and human connection involved in performing their roles. However, many observers are increasingly concerned that the advent of AI would reduce the extent of human interactions during learning. For instance, in the past, if you were stuck on a problem, it is likely that you would consult your teachers or friends. In that process, you will build valuable skills such as communication to express your queries succinctly and situational awareness to approach others at the right time. Ultimately, education is not solely about learning technical knowledge from books, but also about soft skills that guide us through life.

Thus far, I have taken a rather pessimistic stance on AI and how it may be detrimental to our learning. However, if wielded judiciously, AI can be a potent boost for our education journey. For one, AI has democratised personalised learning and reduced the barriers to entry for a tailored learning experience. In the past, if a student wished to have a lesson curated specifically for him, the most likely option would be to hire a personal tutor who would cost a significant sum of money. In contrast, with the advent of AI now, AI can generate materials for the student on-demand at a fraction of the cost of a personal tutor. While some may remain sceptical about the accuracy of the information provided, it is undeniable that the value AI provides compared to its cost and speed is nothing short of revolutionary to the education landscape. From teaching concepts to generating practice questions, AI has redefined how we view tailored learning and made it possible for every student to have a 24/7 “teacher” focused solely on his learning.

Furthermore, AI has also allowed learning to be democratised across geographical boundaries as well! I distinctly recall the time when I was studying for the International Chemistry Olympiad and certain countries had amazing resources but in their native languages (e.g. China and Russia). Instead of laboriously copying and pasting the text into Google Translate, AI can easily convert the entire pdf from one language to another in mere seconds. Indeed, while the internet has provided us with access to a wealth of resources, much of this remains locked behind language barriers—AI is the key that we require to truly unlock this valuable information and equalise resources between countries.

Before I end my discussion on how AI reshapes the way we see education, perhaps it is apt to touch on the most valuable qualities that we may require in this AI era. Indeed, with AI disrupting industries and the way we learn, what then are the most important skills that we should possess? In my opinion, it is adaptability and willingness to learn new tools. AI has provided us with a more efficient toolbelt to solve a variety of problems, but as with any tool, there will always be a learning curve initially—steep or gentle. I recall the time when I wanted to create professional-looking Chemistry notes, and a new open-source tool called Typst proved to be a powerful alternative to the more commonly used LaTeX. I believe that such a trend will dominate the next few decades—AI has made it easier for us to curate specialized tools for any one particular task, and the individuals who will thrive in this era are those that are willing to constantly pick up these new tools. The question, then, is simple. Are you hungry enough to keep learning?
